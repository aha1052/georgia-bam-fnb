Georgia BAM Project: F&B Business Plan

Envoy Rooftop Bistro Cafe
Last Updated · 2026.11.16

0 Executive Summary

0.1 Business Direction

The Georgia BAM F&B Project will operate a bistro cafe on the Envoy rooftop. The daytime menu will focus on coffee and desserts, with Roasted Chicken Plate, wine and draft beer served in the evening. The aim is to provide a place where tourists and local customers can relax or dine while enjoying views of Old Tbilisi.

Collaboration with Envoy Hostel and Tours offers opportunities for joint marketing and promotions. Rooftop photographs, map searches and recommendations from accommodation providers and guides will help attract customers from outside Envoy.

0.2 Operations Suited to the Space

Gas cannot be used on the rooftop, the kitchen is small and there is no separate storeroom. Deliveries must also pass through a sloping alley and reach the third floor without a lift. Oven-roasted chicken will be the main meal, with shared sides and sauces. The bar will handle coffee, alcoholic drinks, Snowflake Bingsu and simple snacks.

There will be 34 seats: 16 on the Open Terrace and 18 in the Covered Area. As usable seating varies with the season and weather, projected low-season revenue is below peak-season revenue. The initial staffing plan comprises three full-time staff, including Tim, supported by seasonal part-time staff. Whether to retain the three-person full-time structure will be reassessed after the first three months of operation.

0.3 Financial Outlook and Conditions for Opening

The planned opening date is 1 April 2027, with operating hours of 12:00–23:00. Opening preparations will proceed once the required power supply and facility conditions are secured.

Projected 2027 revenue is USD 104,295, EBITDA is USD (245), and operating profit is USD (2,683). Net profit after tax, including deposit interest income of USD 3,344, is USD 661. The projected year-end cash balance is USD 49,202. Operating profit is forecast to turn positive in 2028.

The cafe is unlikely to generate an operating profit in its first year. Customer acquisition and menu costs will need to be monitored, with adjustments to part-time staffing, purchasing quantities and capital expenditure.

1 Business Overview

1.1 Role within the Georgia BAM Project

The F&B business will provide a place for Envoy Hostel guests and tour customers to rest and dine, while also serving tourists from outside Envoy and local customers. Introducing the rooftop during hostel check-in, guest information and pre- or post-tour arrangements can create opportunities for meal and gathering reservations.

Tim will lead the training of local staff and establish recipes, service routines and store management practices. A core team will be maintained so local staff can continue working and build operational experience.

2 Location and Customer Acquisition

2.1 Advantages and Constraints of the Envoy Location

The views of Old Tbilisi and the historic building provide reasons for customers to visit the rooftop. Coordination with Envoy Hostel and Tours can support customer information, cafe introductions, and meal or gathering reservations. Planned monthly rent is USD 1,102 in 2027 and USD 1,314 in 2028–2029.

Customers must walk up a sloping alley and climb to the third floor without a lift. The rooftop is not immediately visible from the street, making it difficult to rely on passing foot traffic alone. Views, menus, prices and directions will be provided before customers visit.

2.2 Foot Traffic Observations

From 4–7 August 2026, ten five-minute observations were made at each of two locations: outside Envoy and on the sulphur bath street, approximately 3–5 minutes away on foot. The average was 4.1 people per five minutes outside Envoy and approximately 43 at the comparison point, based on the recorded figures. Some five-minute observations outside Envoy recorded no pedestrians.

A bistro cafe overlooking Old Tbilisi, serving coffee and desserts during the day and Roasted Chicken Plate, wine and draft beer in the evening. In line with Envoy’s Accessible Boutique direction, the cafe will maintain a comfortable atmosphere and reasonable prices.

Rooftop views will attract visits, while Roasted Chicken Plate and Snowflake Bingsu will be promoted as signature menu items.

4 View Promotion and Photo Spot

4.1 Information Before a Visit

Maps and social media will show actual daytime, sunset and night views, seating, signature items and prices. Photographs will explain the route from main tourist walking routes to the building entrance and rooftop.

4.2 Photo Spot and Circulation

A position that captures both the view and the visitor will be selected, with framing and lighting decided after testing smartphone photographs. “Tbilisi, Right Now” remains a wording option for consideration with the design. The installation will keep dining seats, serving routes and stair access clear.

4.3 Photo Sharing and Reviews

Photo-sharing benefits will be tested within the existing marketing budget, with a defined unit cost and conditions. Google Maps reviews will be separate from photo-sharing benefits, and genuine customers will be asked for honest reviews without incentives.[7] Consent will be obtained before customer photographs are reused on the cafe’s social media or website.

5.2 Chicken and Snowflake Bingsu Development

Roasted Chicken Plate is a single-serving meal. The platter combines chicken and sides for 2–3 people to share. Original, Soy Garlic, Spicy Yangnyeom and Cheese flavours and portion sizes will be tested, with combinations suited to draft beer and wine.

Recipes will record chicken weight, cooking yield, sauce and side quantities, serving size and cost. Simultaneous chicken and side cooking, including orders from several tables, will be tested to determine preparation quantities, oven sequencing and service times.

Snowflake Bingsu will be promoted as the signature dessert, with a peak-season target of nine servings per day. Its recipe, portion and presentation will be standardised, and the workflow will allow preparation alongside coffee orders. Ingredient storage and washing space will be included in the bar layout.

5.4 Pricing and Cost Checks

Planned prices will be retained while Tim’s menu tests establish actual serving sizes and ingredient costs. The financial plan’s 33% food and beverage cost ratio is an overall average assumption, which will be checked against individual menu costs and the expected sales mix.

Checks will cover cooked chicken weights, sides and sauces; bingsu ingredient use and waste; and actual saleable keg volume and draft beer losses. Prices or menu combinations will be adjusted after considering the contribution from each item and its preparation and storage requirements. Set and discounted prices will be assessed on the amount remaining after ingredient costs and card processing fees.

5.5 Coffee, Wine and Draft Beer

Coffee service will focus on consistently prepared basic espresso and milk-based drinks. Beans will be selected by comparing taste, supply price, delivery, training and equipment support terms. Additional signature drinks remain development options to be considered within the current average selling price and volume assumptions.

Georgian wines by the glass and bottle will be selected to complement the food. The initial range will reflect storage space, sales speed after opening a bottle and wastage. Draft beer is a core offering, alongside bottled and canned beer. Space, power and delivery access for kegs, cooling and cleaning equipment will need to be checked.

5.6 Small Meal and Gathering Reservations

Reservations for Hostel and Tours customers and outside small groups will use combinations of the current Chicken Platter, sides and drinks. Numbers, menus and arrival times will be agreed in advance, within the capacity to handle regular orders at the same time.

Trial operations will determine group sizes and arrival intervals that allow the small kitchen to handle both regular orders and group meals.

6 Space and Operating Plan

6.1 Power and Facilities

Gas is not permitted on the rooftop, so cooking, coffee, washing and bingsu equipment must use electricity. Securing additional power and confirming that the required equipment can operate simultaneously are prerequisites for this operating plan.

The minimum power capacity that must be allocated to F&B on the rooftop is 35kW. Simultaneous demand will be checked by adding heating and cooling, hot water and ventilation to equipment consumption, then comparing this with the building’s available supply, circuits and installation costs. Available supply requires confirmation, and menu and equipment orders will be finalised accordingly.

The design review is based on a kitchen of approximately 7.5 square metres and a bar approximately 5,200mm long. Equipment door clearance, staff passages, washing and food dispatch areas will be measured, and hood, extraction, water supply and drainage conditions checked. If the required power cannot be secured, the opening schedule and operating plan will need to be reconsidered.

6.2 Seating and Seasonal Operations

The 34 seats comprise 16 on the Open Terrace and 18 in the Covered Area. All seating will be used in peak season, while winter and adverse-weather use will depend on heating and wind protection in the Covered Area. Whether all 18 seats can be used in winter requires on-site confirmation.

The financial plan assumes low-season revenue at 35% of the peak-season level. Seasonal indices are planning assumptions to be tested against usable seats and customer response. Additional heating will be assessed by comparing installation costs, power requirements and monthly operating costs with expected revenue.

6.3 Deliveries and Storage

Food, equipment and supplies must be transported through a sloping alley and up to the third floor without a lift. There is no separate storeroom outside the kitchen, limiting bulk purchasing and long-term storage.

Stair widths, turning points, doorways and packaged equipment dimensions will be checked before shipment. Delivery points and handling arrangements for ingredients, kegs and supplies will be agreed with suppliers. Actual delivery and carrying costs will be checked against the budget once supply terms are known.

The layout will show where and how much chilled, frozen and dry stock, kegs and supplies can be stored. Order sizes and delivery frequency will reflect available space, and menu combinations requiring additional dedicated ingredients will be limited. Preparation quantities and ordering frequency will be adjusted to actual sales.

Maintaining three full-time staff, including Tim, is preferable for consistent operating quality and continuity, but this will be reassessed after the first three months of operation. Peak- and regular-season part-time staffing comprises 48 kitchen-assistant hours and 35 bar-assistant hours per week. The low-season budget contains no separate part-time staffing.

A weekly roster will test whether 12:00–23:00 trading can be covered alongside preparation, closing, breaks and days off. Time for the Manager’s administrative work and cover for Kitchen and Bar days off are also required. Cross-training and trial operations will address gaps; any gaps that cannot be resolved within budget will need to be costed separately.

If demand is below plan, part-time hours, deployment and preparation quantities will be reviewed first. Whether to retain the three-person full-time structure will also be reviewed against early operating results.

6.5 Orders, Service and Operating Controls

Counter ordering and self-pickup are the initial approach for drinks. Table service for evening meals and additional alcohol orders will be decided after testing bar, kitchen and seating circulation. Queues and serving routes will be arranged to avoid conflicts with stairs and the photo spot.

The hostel common areas, cafe trading area and outside customer access routes will be clearly identified. When group bookings overlap with regular service, seating, kitchen preparation and building information will be coordinated with Hostel and Tours staff.

Menu specifications will focus on portions, photographs, service times and storage conditions for chicken, sides, bingsu and drinks. Opening, closing, hygiene, stock and reconciliation procedures will be kept simple for both full-time and part-time staff. POS sales, cash, cards, bank receipts and stock will be reconciled, and actual costs, working hours, profit and cash balances reviewed monthly.

7 Customer Acquisition and Marketing

7.1 Cooperation with Hostel and Tours

Cooperation with Hostel and Tours requires sufficient discussion in advance. Subject to agreement, discussions will cover providing cafe information to Hostel and Tours staff before opening and enabling them to explain joint promotions and discount benefits.

The agreed approach will determine how cafe information and promotions are introduced during hostel check-in, guest information and pre- or post-tour arrangements. Joint promotions will proceed once eligible customers, periods, cost allocation and settlement arrangements have been agreed.

Hostel guest use and Tours referrals and reservations will be recorded to assess how this cooperation translates into sales.

7.2 Attracting Tourists from Outside Envoy

Google Maps and the cafe page will display actual views, chicken, bingsu and seating photographs, together with menus, prices and opening hours. Photographs will explain the route from main tourist walking routes to the building entrance and third floor.

Social media will show food and prices alongside views, sunsets and night scenes. Nearby accommodation providers and guides will receive the same material and reservation contact details. Direction requests, enquiries, visits and reservations will be monitored alongside online responses.

7.3 Local Customers and Low-Season Demand

Roasted Chicken Plate, Snowflake Bingsu, Georgian wine, beer and space for gatherings will be introduced to local customers and foreign residents. Small meal and social bookings and repeat visits will help assess demand outside the tourist peak season.

Winter operations will be based on heating, wind protection and usable seating in the Covered Area. Small meals, gatherings and seasonal menus will be promoted, while the effects of adjusting preparation, purchasing, menu composition and opening hours during quieter periods will be reviewed. Changes to opening hours will also be reflected in financial assumptions and customer information.

7.4 Initial Execution and Cost Control

The financial plan applies opening Ramp-up factors from April to July. Customer response and referral channels will be reviewed weekly, with early attention to access information, menu explanations and service times.

The monthly USD 200 marketing budget includes the existing welcome-drink allocation. Photo-sharing benefits and advertising will also be planned within this budget. Any separate referral commission or additional advertising will be costed and reflected in the financial plan.

Reviews will be checked and answered within 24–48 hours, and recurring issues addressed in operations. Customer numbers and sales by referral channel will be compared with benefit and advertising costs to plan the following month’s spending.

8 Business Viability and Key Risks

8.1 Operating Adjustments and Constraints

If sales fall below plan, part-time hours, preparation and purchasing quantities will be adjusted, and the timing of additional equipment and supplies purchases reconsidered. Results from joint Hostel and Tours promotions and outside customer acquisition will be reviewed together.

Sales, workload and service quality during the first three months will inform a review of the three-person full-time structure and part-time staffing. Winter seating constraints, deliveries and storage, and small-kitchen capacity will also be considered.

8.3 Decisions Based on Initial Results

After opening, the business will track daytime and evening customers, Hostel and Tours use, outside visits and menu orders alongside the overall peak-season target of 50 customers per day. Distinguishing customer-number variances from spend-per-customer variances will help determine the response.

The financial scenarios show the cost savings needed if customer numbers fall by 10% or 20%. The scope to achieve these savings through part-time staffing, purchasing and other spending will be checked separately. Early results will inform staffing and operating scope, including whether to retain the three-person full-time structure.

At the end of June, April–June customer numbers, orders, costs, working hours and cash will be reviewed together to adjust menus, reservations and part-time staffing for the remaining peak season.

10.2 Trial Operation Completion Criteria

Service times, quality, power use, washing and staff workload will be checked when chicken, sides, coffee, alcohol and bingsu orders overlap. Preparation quantities and reservation limits will be set after checking whether 34-seat service and bookings fit actual kitchen and bar capacity.

Full-time and part-time rosters will be tested against preparation, trading, closing, breaks and days off. Ingredient, keg and supply deliveries and storage, hostel common areas and customer access routes will also be checked.

Cafe information and promotion materials agreed with Hostel and Tours will be prepared, and access from the building entrance and ordering information tested. Unresolved facility or staffing gaps and additional costs will be addressed before the official opening.
